# HexWiki compiler guide

This is the complete writing contract for a source-bounded Open Knowledge
Format (OKF) wiki. It governs model-written notes and the navigation that joins
them, and it is the single source of truth for how notes are typed, written,
linked, indexed, and logged. Deterministic source guides and PDF-page gateways
are generated by the engine and are never model-editable.

The wiki is an **OKF bundle**: plain markdown files whose YAML front matter
carries queryable metadata, with relative links forming the knowledge graph and
the file path serving as each note's identity.

This guide is domain-neutral. It describes the *shape* of the wiki, never its
subject matter. The subject matter comes only from the staged source text.

## What this wiki is for

A source-bounded wiki is not a summary and not an encyclopedia. It is a
**navigable, auditable reconstruction of one bounded slice of a source** — built
so that a later reader can answer three questions about any statement in it:

1. What did the source actually say?
2. Where exactly in the source does it say that, and how good is that support?
3. What is the source's own argument, and where does that argument run ahead of
   its evidence?

Everything below follows from those three questions. A wiki that reads well but
cannot answer (2) and (3) has failed.

## Epistemic status and permitted inputs

Every semantic note is an unverified draft. Inclusion means only that the
material occurs in the locked scope. Never turn repetition into corroboration,
reported speech into fact, an author's interpretation into consensus, or an
absence in the selected pages into an absence everywhere.

Generation may use only:

- the current run's immutable staged source under `_ingest/`;
- exact current-run PDF-page gateways under `sources/pdf-pages/`;
- the current run's machine-validated survey under `_plan/`; and
- notes already written in the same fresh candidate.

Never consult a prior wiki, gold/reference wiki, comparison, persistent memory,
shared skill, website, or outside factual knowledge while writing.

If a `WIKI_GUIDE.md` file exists inside the wiki directory, it is this guide: a
structural file. Never edit it, never link to it, never list it in `index.md`.

## Note vocabulary and locations

The wiki has a **fixed note-type vocabulary**. Every note declares its type in
front matter, and the type must match its location. Do not invent new types or
folders, do not rename these, and do not create aliases or redirects. Each
subject has one canonical home.

| Location | `type:` | Function |
|---|---|---|
| `overview.md` | `Overview` | Orient a new reader to the whole scope and its central question. |
| `reading-guide.md` | `Reading Guide` | Give concrete reading, lookup, and audit paths. |
| `chapters/<nn>-<slug>.md` | `Chapter` | Reconstruct one top-level scoped division's architecture. |
| `sections/<nn>-<slug>.md` | `Section` | Follow each source subdivision in source order. |
| `cases/<slug>.md` | `Case Dossier` | Record one narrated incident, record, observation, experiment, or worked example. |
| `concepts/<slug>.md` | `Concept` | Explain a substantive, methodological, or epistemic idea. |
| `people/<slug>.md` | `Person` | Describe the author in scope, or collect other names by role. |
| `synthesis/<slug>.md` | `Synthesis` | Analyze argument, claims, recurring features, and controls. |
| `synthesis/open-questions.md` | `Open Question` | State material questions the scope cannot settle. |
| `sources/<slug>.md`, `reference/<slug>.md` | `Source Guide` | Deterministic provenance, scope, and locator infrastructure. |

Filenames are kebab-case. **The file path is the note's identity** — never
rename a note casually; links are the graph.

Folder `index.md` files and the root `index.md` are catalogs and carry no front
matter. `log.md` is append-only. `_schema/`, `reports/`, and `audit/` are engine
infrastructure and are never model-written.

## Completeness rules — what must exist

The accepted survey inventory under `_plan/` is the build contract. These rules
decide *how many* notes there are, and they override any instinct to
consolidate. In a source-bounded wiki the number of notes is determined by the
source's own structure, not by an editor's sense of importance.

- **Chapter / Section.** Mirror the source's own divisions. Every scoped chapter
  gets a `Chapter` note acting as a hub: what the chapter argues, its ordered
  list of sections, what it establishes, and what it leaves open. Every section
  the source marks off — by heading, by a break in the argument, or by a clear
  shift of subject — gets a `Section` note, in the source's own order.

- **Case Dossier — one per distinct narrated episode, with no importance
  threshold.** If the source narrates a specific incident, experiment, record,
  transaction, encounter, observation, or worked example — however briefly — it
  gets its own dossier. A three-sentence passing mention with no supporting
  apparatus still gets a dossier; the dossier then says so in its evidence
  limits, and that statement is itself valuable.

  **This is the rule most often broken.** Do **not** roll several episodes into
  one thematic note: a reader looking up a specific incident by name must land
  on a note about that incident. Where the source itself presents a set of
  records as a compilation or catalog rather than as individual episodes, that
  compilation gets one dossier describing the set, its span, and its provenance.

  A short source deserves short notes, not padding, and a bibliography entry, a
  date, a name, or a word's origin is never promoted into an incident merely to
  satisfy a count. Architecture floors belong to the profile; a null floor is
  permission to follow the source's shape, never permission to omit the list.

- **Concept — one per idea the source *uses*, including the ones it never
  names.** Cover three kinds:
  1. *Substantive* concepts: the phenomena, mechanisms, categories, and motifs
     the source discusses.
  2. *Methodological* concepts: how the source reasons — the comparison it
     performs, the inference it draws, the criteria it applies. State both what
     the method licenses and what it does not.
  3. *Epistemic* concepts: the load-bearing assumptions. Where a source's
     argument depends on a claim it never argues for, that claim is a concept
     note. Where the same material admits several incompatible readings and the
     source does not settle between them, that underdetermination is a concept
     note.

  A recurring effect, pattern, or property described across several episodes
  gets its own concept note rather than being folded into a broader one.
  Splitting is the default: two mechanisms that co-occur are still two notes if
  the source treats them as distinct, and a reader searching for one of them
  must find a note whose title is that thing.

  Every concept note carries **`## Instances in scope`**: the complete list of
  episodes exhibiting it, each linked, one bullet each with a clause on how it
  exhibits the concept. Complete means complete — find them by searching the
  case dossiers for the concept's vocabulary, not by recalling the ones you
  happened to read most recently. A note that lists two of its six episodes
  tells a reader the phenomenon is rarer than the wiki's own dossiers show,
  which is a false statement about the evidence.

  **Every rule has exactly one canonical home.** A guardrail restated four times
  in four weakened forms is a guardrail nobody can cite. When several notes
  depend on the same methodological move or epistemic assumption, one note owns
  it — states it as a named rule, in full — and the others link to that note
  instead of paraphrasing it.

- **Person.** In a source-bounded wiki most named people are *roles*, not
  subjects: an author cited once, a witness in one incident, an official who
  issued one ruling. Do not give each a thin page.
  - The **author of the scoped source** gets one note covering their role in
    *this* material specifically — what they compile, what they interpret, where
    they editorialize, and which of their moves a reader should audit. It is not
    a biography.
  - Named people who appear only as roles are collected into **roster notes**
    grouped by the broad kind of role they play — for example the historical
    authors and intermediaries a source transmits material through, and the
    modern witnesses and investigators whose records it cites. **Prefer a small
    number of broad rosters over many narrow ones**: group by kind of role, not
    by occupation, era, or discipline. A handful of rosters covering everyone is
    correct; a roster per profession fragments the graph and buries the people
    who matter.
  - A roster note is a table — Name, Role in this scope, linked appearance, What
    to be careful about — plus prose on what the group has in common. Every
    person named in the scoped text appears in exactly one roster row or in
    their own note. Never add biographical knowledge absent from scope.
  - A person gets their own note only if they carry substantial independent
    weight across several episodes or sections.

- **Synthesis.** Analysis *about* the source, never a restatement of it.
  Maintain five artifacts:
  - `argument-map.md` — the source's inference chain as an ordered list of
    steps, each labelled with what supports it, what it assumes, and the point
    of evidential overreach.
  - `claim-evidence-matrix.md` — a literal table with Claim, Owner, In-scope
    evidence, Evidential limit, and linked Notes.
  - `motif-matrix.md` — a literal table with one Case Dossier per row and one
    recurring feature per column, each cell marked or blank, plus an explicit
    note on how the set of episodes was selected. A marked cell is a claim about
    that case's cited page; when in doubt, leave it blank. Prose about the
    patterns goes below the table and does not replace it — the grid is the
    retrieval artifact.
  - `critical-reading.md` — the argument's genuine strengths set against the
    controls it lacks, naming the specific methodological risks that apply, such
    as selection effects, heterogeneous source quality, non-independence between
    sources, transmission or translation drift, and compression of evidence into
    summary.
  - `open-questions.md` — what the scope leaves unresolved: analytic questions
    about the material, what would discriminate between competing readings, what
    evidence would settle a disputed point. **Not** a to-do list of wiki
    coverage gaps; ingestion backlog belongs in `log.md`.

- **Overview and Reading Guide.** `overview.md` states the scope's organizing
  question, its argument in a handful of numbered points, what it establishes
  versus what it leaves open, the neutral-reading rule this wiki applies, and
  paths to the Chapter, synthesis, and source guides. `reading-guide.md` gives
  concrete paths *through* the wiki: a first-read order, an audit path from
  matrix to note to source gateway, a lookup workflow for finding a specific
  episode or concept, the PDF page-map path, and an explicit statement of the
  scope boundary so a reader does not mistake the wiki for coverage of the whole
  work.

## Front matter: OKF v0.2

Every semantic note starts with a valid YAML block like this:

```yaml
---
type: Concept
title: "Exact H1 title"
description: "A retrieval-oriented description containing at least eight useful words."
tags: [scope-id, concept]
semantic_note: true
status: draft
pdf_pages: [12, 13]
sources:
  - { resource: "sources/pdf-pages/page-0012.md", locator: "PDF page 12" }
  - { resource: "sources/pdf-pages/page-0013.md", locator: "PDF page 13" }
generated: { by: "hexwiki", mode: "model-compiled" }
---
```

Rules:

- `type` exactly matches the location table.
- `title` exactly equals the first H1 after front matter.
- `description` is 1–2 sentences **optimized for retrieval**, at least eight
  words. Retrieval tools use this field to decide whether the note answers a
  query, so it must carry the concrete names, dates, and places a searcher would
  type, plus the note's evidential character. Do not merely restate the title.
- `tags` is a non-empty list of lowercase kebab-case tokens: the folder, the
  scope slug, and 2–4 topical keywords.
- `semantic_note: true` and `status: draft` are constants. Machine-compiled
  notes are unverified drafts by construction; the writing agent never raises a
  status.
- **Never write a `verified` key.** OKF v0.2 types `verified` as a list of
  confirmation events — who checked this note, and when — and reads the
  *absence* of the key as "nobody has". Nothing in this pipeline checks a note
  against anything, so the honest encoding is omission. `verified: true` because
  a note looks good is a fabrication; `verified: false` or `verified: []` is
  subtler and still wrong, because it puts a value where a consumer looks for
  confirmations.
- `pdf_pages` lists only page numbers actually used by the note, uniquely and in
  ascending order.
- `sources` is a non-empty list of mappings, one per distinct source unit the
  note draws on. Every mapping has a `resource` pointing at the canonical
  gateway you actually cited in the body, not at a summary of it.
- Every page in `pdf_pages` is linked in the body beside the claim it supports.
- **Quote `title` and `description`.** YAML treats a bare ` #` as a comment
  start and silently truncates the value — a real hazard whenever prose contains
  record numbers ("case #12481") or hashtags, and invisible until a retrieval
  tool reads the broken value. Escape inner double quotes as `\"`.

## Note body

After front matter, every semantic note has an H1 exactly matching `title`, a
bold one-sentence orientation, sections appropriate to its type, `## Connections`
with links to real related notes, and `## Sources` with direct relative links to
the exact PDF-page gateways.

```markdown
# Note Title

**One-sentence summary of what this is and why it matters in the scoped
material.**

## <Reported account | What the source says>

Neutral prose: what the source states, attributed to the source. Names, dates,
places, numbers, quantities — the specifics a reader would look up.

## Source chain

Who reported it to whom, and through what intermediaries the scoped source
received it. Where the source gives a citation, name it and link the
citation-apparatus note.

## Use in the argument

What work this note's material does for the source's case — what it is offered
as evidence *for*, and where it sits in the argument.

## Evidence limits

What in-scope support exists and — explicitly — what does not: no corroborating
witness, no named investigator, no primary document, no measurement, no date, a
single intermediary, a translation of a translation. Absence of support is a
finding and must be stated, not left implied.

## Connections

- [Related Note](../concepts/related-note.md) — one line on how it relates.

## Sources

- *Work Title* (Author, Year), ch. 3 "Chapter Name" (pdf pp. 44–46) — what this
  contributed. [PDF p. 44](../sources/pdf-pages/page-0044.md)
```

Use the headings that fit the note type — a Concept note has no "Source chain";
a Section note has an ordered list of what it covers. `## Sources` is required on
every note.

Use ordinary relative Markdown links. From `cases/example.md`, PDF page 12 is
`../sources/pdf-pages/page-0012.md`; from a root note it is
`sources/pdf-pages/page-0012.md`. Every link must resolve. Cite the most precise
locator the source provides and link the immutable extracted page directly, so a
claim can be checked in one click. Page numbers as bare prose are not provenance.

Write only what the cited pages support. Preserve names, dates, locations,
quantities, sequence, and uncertainty exactly as given. Quote sparingly,
paraphrase faithfully, and keep quotation marks only around words actually on the
page.

## Epistemic discipline

The wiki keeps four things visibly distinct. Blurring them is the most serious
error a writer can make, because it launders a claim into a fact.

1. **Reported account** — what a witness, record, or document is said to state.
2. **Source chain** — who transmitted it, and how far it is from the event.
3. **Author interpretation** — what the scoped source concludes from it.
4. **Wiki inference** — anything the wiki observes that the source did not say.
   Label these explicitly; keep them rare and modest.

Rules:

- Attribute contested claims: "the chapter reports", "the witness is said to
  have", "the author argues". Never assert a contested claim in the wiki's own
  voice.
- **State only what the scoped source supports.** Never add facts from general
  knowledge, however certain — a claim that cannot be traced to the staged
  source does not belong in a note. This includes plausible dates, full names,
  and background context the source omits.
- Where the source declines to settle a question, the wiki declines too. Record
  the competing readings and the fact that the scope offers no test between
  them. Do not manufacture a verdict, in either direction.
- Resemblance is not identity; recurrence is not cause; a vivid account is not a
  well-sourced one. Where the source's argument leans on one of these, say so on
  the relevant concept or synthesis note.
- Never cite anything outside the declared scope, including other parts of the
  same work.

## Case Dossier

A Case Dossier uses this order:

1. `## Reported account` — attributed narration with every specific the scope
   supplies.
2. `## Source chain` — who reports what through which intermediary, and any
   apparatus entry that applies.
3. `## Use in the argument` — what the source offers the episode as evidence
   for.
4. `## Evidence limits`.
5. `## Connections`.
6. `## Sources`.

The Evidence limits section is **mandatory** and names concrete absences: no
second witness, no named investigator, no primary record, no measurement, no
date, an anonymous intermediary, a translation, or only an authorial summary —
whichever the scoped pages actually warrant. Do not invent an absent control that
the pages provide.

## Concept

Every Concept note defines the idea, explains how the source uses it, and
carries `## Instances in scope` linking every Case Dossier that genuinely
exhibits it. Then apply its kind-specific guardrail:

- A **substantive** concept ends with `## Evidence status` — for each instance,
  whether it is *alleged* (asserted in a report) or *validated* (supported by a
  document, measurement, or named investigator in scope), and where the
  evidential chain breaks. Never let a repeated allegation read as
  corroboration.
- A **methodological or epistemic** concept ends with `## What this licenses and
  what it does not`, as labelled lists: *Supports* — the weak form of the claim
  the material actually bears; *Does not support* — the strong form a reader is
  likely to slide into, written as a sentence someone might believe; *Controls
  absent in scope* — the specific ones missing (no sampling frame, no comparison
  set, sources not independent of one another, no measurement, no named
  investigator, unequal reliability across eras). If the first two lists could be
  swapped without anyone noticing, they are too vague to be a guardrail.

The weak/strong pair is the whole point. *Recurrence across eras* is weak and
supported; *therefore one persistent cause* is strong and is not. *These accounts
share a motif* is weak; *therefore they describe the same kind of thing* is
strong. Write both halves as sentences a reader could believe, and say plainly
which one the scope does not reach.

Similar accounts may support a claim of recurring description; they do not by
themselves prove one persistent cause, and repetition may reflect
non-independent transmission.

## Chapter and Section

The Chapter note reconstructs the organizing question and ordered inference. It
links every Section in source order, separates what the scope establishes from
what it leaves open, and identifies the hinge where an inference needs more than
the preceding material supplies.

A Section note follows its pages in source order. It explains the division's
function, links the episodes and concepts introduced there, connects upward to
the Chapter, and states what the division does not establish.

## Linking and navigation

- Relative markdown links: `[Note](../concepts/some-note.md)`.
- Link the first mention of another wiki note in a body, and link liberally —
  the link graph is the wiki's main value and, in OKF terms, the knowledge graph
  itself.
- A link to a note that does not exist is a lint error: create the note or drop
  the link. Never create alias or redirect notes — one entity, one note.
- **No orphans.** Every note must be reachable from `index.md` and linked from at
  least one other note. Hub notes (Chapter, Section, roster, matrix) are what
  make this true: they link down to their members, and members link back up.
- Titles are unique across the wiki.

## index.md and folder catalogs

- Root `index.md` opens with a short orientation block (2–4 sentences, no
  heading): what the wiki covers, from which source and scope, and the caveat
  that inclusion is not verification. Then one `##` section per folder, one
  bullet per note: `- [Note Title](cases/some-case.md) — one-line hook.`
- The root catalog lists every semantic note, all source and reference guides,
  and `sources/pdf-pages/index.md`, each exactly once, using paths relative to
  the root.
- Each folder carries its own `index.md` with the same bullet form, listing every
  note in that folder exactly once.
- Update the catalog in the same operation that creates a note.
- Catalog files carry NO front matter.

## Repair and deletion

Read before editing. A review repair re-reads both the note and the gateway it
will cite, makes the smallest supported correction, then re-reads both again.
Deleting an unsupportable claim is preferred to leaving it standing. Never repair
a note by changing source infrastructure.

Use `delete_file` only for a model-written duplicate, wrong-path note, or stray
artifact. Never create deletion markers, shell scripts, probe files, aliases, or
temporary notes. No model-facing stage creates or materially rewrites more than
the one or two artifacts explicitly named in its instruction.

## log.md

Append-only, no front matter. Add concise entries at the end, newest last:

```text
- YYYY-MM-DD [writer] stage: what changed and why
```

Use the date and writer tag given for the operation — never copy one from this
example or from an earlier line. The local run audit is the authoritative
operational record; the wiki log is a reader-facing change summary and never
claims verification.

## Ingestion workflow

1. Read `index.md` and this guide.
2. Read the **full** staged source text for the scope, end to end, before
   writing anything.
3. **Inventory first.** Enumerate every functional unit the scope contains —
   sections, episodes, concepts, people, claims, citation entries — before
   creating any note. Completeness is decided at this step; a unit missed here is
   missed permanently.
4. Write notes family by family: structure first (Chapter, Section), then Case
   Dossiers, then Concepts, then People, then Synthesis, then the navigation
   notes last, when there is something real to navigate.
5. Update folder catalogs and `index.md`; append one `log.md` entry.
6. Re-read the inventory and confirm every unit has a note. Anything left over is
   either written now or recorded in `synthesis/open-questions.md` with the
   reason.
7. Fix obvious extraction errors from the raw text silently — a word split across
   a line break such as `"ex ample"`, or a soft hyphen left mid-word — and never
   propagate them into notes. Record systematic anomalies on the provenance note
   rather than altering the immutable gateway.

## Quality bar

- Every note carries valid front matter with `semantic_note: true`,
  `status: draft`, no `verified` key, and a non-empty `sources` list.
- Notes are self-contained: a reader landing on any note understands it without
  reading the source.
- **Prefer specific facts — dates, places, names, quantities, record numbers —
  over vague summary.** A dossier that says "a witness reported strange effects"
  where the source gives a name, a date, and three specific effects has failed.
- Length follows the evidence: roughly 150–400 words is typical, brief dossiers
  for briefly-narrated episodes are correct, and no note is padded to reach a
  length.
- Every `## Sources` bullet ends with a dash and what that source contributed.
- No filler, no meta-commentary about the ingestion process, no first person.
- Run the linter after every operation and fix all errors.
